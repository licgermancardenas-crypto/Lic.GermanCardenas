The reading that spread after 2025 was simple: PRO collapsed in Recoleta and Palermo, its stronghold. Measured in percentage points, that's true. As a diagnosis for a campaign, it's misleading.

## The problem with percentage points

If every neighbourhood loses the **same share** of its voters, the neighbourhood where the party had the most votes loses the most points. A 40% that halves drops 20 points; a 20% that halves drops 10. The map of "where it fell the most" ends up being, to a large extent, a map of where it had the most.

A campaign built on that reading goes out to defend the wrong neighbourhoods: it puts resources where the fall is large in points but not different in proportion, and neglects what actually changed.

## What we measured instead

The right question isn't how much each neighbourhood fell, but **what share of its voters** the party kept in each place. That can't be observed directly: the vote is secret and we only see totals per polling station. To estimate it we used ecological inference, with **three independent methods**, because a single ecological-inference method is too easy to bend.

The database crosses fifteen elections harmonised with the 2022 census by census tract, the voter roll and urban layers, at the level of electoral circuit (167) and polling station. Nothing was accepted without passing three filters: correction for multiple comparisons, a control for social composition and a case-by-case fragility test.

## What we found

- All three methods agree: PRO kept **29.9% of its votes**.
- Retention is **even across the city**. There is no territorial refuge where the party held up better.
- None of **73 census indicators** explains who stayed.

> Territory explains almost all of the level of the vote and almost none of the change.

With out-of-sample validation, territory explains the level of the vote with an R² of **0.935** and the change with an R² of **−0.05**: worse than using no model at all.

## So what does change by neighbourhood?

Not how much left, but **who it went to**. The territorial story isn't in the loss but in who captured that vote, and there the differences are useful for planning.

## Why it matters to say it in time

A brand diagnosis and a territorial one lead to opposite campaigns. If the problem is territorial, you reinforce the ground game in the neighbourhoods that were "lost". If it's the brand, that spending recovers nothing: what needs work is the message and the offer, and territory tells you who to compete against in each area.
